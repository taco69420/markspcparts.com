## What These Are

To put this in context: 9000 MT/s CUDIMMs in 2026 are some of the fastest consumer DDR5 money can buy. The CK (Clock Driver) in CUDIMM re-buffers the clock signal on the stick itself, which lets the memory controller push way higher frequencies than it could with a standard UDIMM. It's the same principle as RDIMMs in servers but adapted for consumer platforms.

![Close-up of G.Skill Trident Z5 CK sticks showing the CUDIMM label](/images/posts/cudimm-9000-400/up-close-cudimm.jpg)

A 32GB DDR5 kit of normal speed costs around what I paid for these. I got 48GB at 9000 MT/s.

## Running CUDIMMs on a Z890 Tomahawk

Here's where it gets interesting. The Z890 Tomahawk WiFi is a 4-slot motherboard — and CUDIMM support here is official, not a hack. MSI's own spec sheet lists this board for CUDIMM overclocking up to 9200+ MT/s at 1 DIMM per channel. The real challenge on four slots isn't "will it even post" — it's signal integrity. More slots on the same trace means more to fight versus a dedicated 2-slot board built around one stick per channel from the start.
I slapped them in.

They run. They run well. I ran them at **8800 MT/s** on the Tomahawk, on four slots instead of two. I did run the full 9000 once, in Gear 4, as a send-off to the board before the kit moved over to a Z890 Apex — but 8800 is where I spent my time. Paired with a Core Ultra 9 285K, the 8800 setup gave me **132 GB/s read bandwidth** at the peak — which is getting close to what I was seeing out of the 6-channel [Dominus Extreme](/blog/dominus-extreme-resurrection/) setup with the [W-3175X](/blog/xeon-w-3175x-270-dollar-monster/). On two sticks, four-slot board, nothing exotic.

Either the 285K has a really strong IMC, the Tomahawk has better traces than people give it credit for, or both. Probably both.
